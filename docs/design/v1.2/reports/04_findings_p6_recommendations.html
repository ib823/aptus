Why we recommend what we recommend

SAP's design philosophy is "configure, don't customize." Every line of custom code creates future maintenance burden, upgrade risk, and dependency on the developer who wrote it.

So when we evaluate each of your requirements, we follow this hierarchy:

  1. Can SAP do this as standard?
    → Use it. Cheapest, safest, upgrade-friendly.
  2. Can we configure SAP to do this?
    → Configure it. Still upgrade-safe, no code.
  3. Can your process adapt to SAP best practice?
    → Recommend the adaptation. Often improves the process; certainly cheaper to maintain.
  4. Is there a trusted add-on?
    → Use the add-on. Maintained by the vendor; your team doesn't carry the support burden.
  5. Can a power user build it?
    → Empower the user. SAP's no-code tools are mature.
  6. Last resort: custom build (in core SAP or on the cloud platform).
    → Build it carefully. Reserved for genuine competitive differentiation.

This is why most of your requirements land in categories 1–2. That isn't us being lazy — that's us protecting your investment in SAP for the next 10 years.
