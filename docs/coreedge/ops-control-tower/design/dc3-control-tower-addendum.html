CoreEdge Console · design addendum · second tranche

Two things Control Tower needs that neither dc2 nor the first tranche contains

The Operations Center tranche settled how a screen carries what its data cannot support. This settles the two concepts specific to governance: a permission that ends by lapsing, and a control that is real but belongs to somebody else.

Context

What is already decided, and stays decided

Everything from the Operations Center tranche applies unchanged. Nothing here re-opens it.

  • The provenance strip — furniture on the table-header fill, claim as headline, inside the card.
  • The dashed absence pill — an absence is not a status and does not get a colour.
  • Numbers carry their basis, and a number that cannot exist renders an em-dash at full size.
  • Thin-not-empty — the strip says "showing all N" so one row cannot read as a broken screen.

One inherited rule matters more here than it did there. Governance screens are censuses, not floors: the portfolio, the connection register and the credential register list every row in scope, with no sampling and no page. That is the opposite of the broker feeds, and the strips must say so — otherwise a reader who learned "these numbers are a floor" on one screen carries that doubt onto a screen where it is not true, and under-trusts a complete count.

E1

A permission that ends only by lapsing

There is no revocation path in this release, and a settled grant cannot be re-decided. So an approved grant ends one way: its expiry passes. Time remaining is not a date in a cell — it is the entire lifecycle control, and it has to read like one.

Expiry is the only ending
There is no revocation control in this release and a settled request cannot be re-decided. An approved grant ends when its expiry passes — which is why a write-granting decision cannot be settled without one.

Time remaining is a runway, not a date. "Ends 8 Sep" requires arithmetic against today; "6 days" does not. The bar makes a short runway visible without reading either. It depletes toward zero rather than filling toward it — the thing being measured is what is left.

"Authorises" is its own column, and it is the honest one. A decision label is not a permission: SANDBOX_ONLY on a PROD grant authorises nothing, and READ_ONLY on a CREATE request authorises a read and not the write that was asked for. The endpoint computes this with the runtime predicates, so the column cannot drift from what the broker will actually do. Rendering the decision alone would leave a reviewer to know the rules from elsewhere.

A lapsed grant keeps its decision chip and loses its authority. It still reads "approved", muted, beside nothing — lapsed. Recolouring the decision would rewrite what was decided; a decision that was made does not un-happen because time passed.

No expiry is an absence, not a blank. On a read grant that is legitimate and gets the dashed pill with its reason. On a settled write grant it cannot occur — the server refuses it — and if one ever appears, it is a defect and must read as one.

This should not exist
A write grant with no expiry cannot be ended — there is no revocation path and it cannot be re-decided. The server refuses to settle one, so a row in this state predates that rule and needs a new, bounded request to replace it.

Designed as a defect, not as a state. It is the one row on this screen that should never appear, so it gets the failure colour and a strip that says what to do — rather than sitting quietly in the list as though it were an ordinary variation.

E2

A control that is real, and is not yours

Rotating a credential and enabling write on a connection are genuine capabilities, gated to the consultant role — so a platform admin, this workspace's owner, receives a 403. Deleting them hides a capability from the person doing oversight; wiring them live ships a button that fails for the only role that can reach the screen.

Rotating and revoking are the builder's actions on their own solution, gated to the consultant role. Control Tower observes them; it does not perform them. The control is shown disabled rather than hidden, because it exists.

Disabled with a reason, in the strip rather than a tooltip. A tooltip requires a hover to discover and is unreachable by keyboard on a disabled control. The strip states where the action lives, permanently, for everyone.

The button keeps its real label. "Rotate or revoke", not "Unavailable". A reviewer needs to know what capability exists and who holds it; renaming the control to describe its own disabled-ness discards exactly that.

Segregation of duties is rendered as a verdict, not an id. The register's job is to let a reviewer confirm the control held. issuer was not an owner is the finding; createdById: u_7f3a is homework. Where the solution is out of scope the cell shows the dashed pill — unknowable, which is not the same as passing.

E3

Where governance differs from operations

ConcernOperations CenterControl Tower
CompletenessA floor — feeds under-reportA census — every row in scope, stated as such
Deactivated connectionsExcluded; no health worth reportingIncluded; still part of the estate
Time frameA window, usually 24hCurrent state, with history in the audit trail
Audiencesupport and adminpartner lead, exec sponsor, PM, admin — not support
ActionsNone; read-only throughoutOne: the grant decision, admin-only

The audience difference is the one to design for. Operations is read by someone on shift who needs the answer in seconds. Control Tower is read by someone reviewing, who needs to be able to reconstruct a judgement — so it spends more words per row, states its verdicts, and links out to where actions live rather than compressing.

Open questions

Not resolved here — each is a product decision.

  • Should the audit trail be filterable by actor? The endpoint accepts the filter. Making it prominent turns a governance record into something that reads as individual monitoring, and that is a policy question rather than a UI one.
  • What is the expiry runway on a read grant? Writes must be bounded; reads may be unbounded and legitimately are. Whether an unbounded read grant deserves any visual weight at all, or is simply normal, is unsettled.
  • Does the portfolio need a "governed" verdict per solution? Ownership completeness and a live credential are both shown. Combining them into one pass/fail badge would be scannable and would also invent a threshold nobody has defined.
