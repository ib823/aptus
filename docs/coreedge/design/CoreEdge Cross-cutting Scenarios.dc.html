CoreEdge · scenario states · Cross-cutting · A4 rows X01–X06

Six states that cut across every role

Rights, phone, question, keyboard, time zone, and the one view built for someone outside ABeam. Every rule established so far holds: one key per app per environment, access per data feed, requester never approver. Invented content carries an example tag.

Disabled ≠ skipped
A disabled Approve is focusable and announces "Approve, unavailable: there's no Prod SAP system yet." Removing it from the order would hide the one sentence that explains the screen.
Rows are data, not links
A five-row table is one tab stop with arrow keys inside it, so Tab still crosses the screen in seven presses rather than forty.
The ring is navy
Focus is not a status. Red is reserved for irreversible actions, so only Revoke and Retire carry a red ring — where the ring matches the commit.
Other states

X05 – "10 Dec" means the end of 10 Dec, in MYT
Promote to Test · as Ikmal, in MYT
Access ends
Access ends 10 Dec 2026, 23:59 MYT (UTC+8)
That is the end of the day you picked, in your time zone. 90 days from today.
A date alone is ambiguous by up to a day, and an access that ends a day early on a Friday evening is a support call on Monday. CoreEdge resolves it once, here, and shows the resolution before you send.
Stored once, in UTC
The instant is 2026-12-10T15:59:59Z. Every screen renders that one instant in the reader's zone, so nobody is converting anything by hand.
Never "in 90 days" alone
Relative time is a helper, never the fact. Expiry, approval and revocation always carry the date, the time and the zone.
The same instant, three readers

Fields, not tables
"12 of 71" is the fact that matters: an app approved for a service is not approved for everything in it. The field list is one click away on every row.
Every line has a person and a date
Who approved it, when, and when it stops. No access here is open-ended, and nothing renews itself.
No actions, deliberately
There is no Revoke button here. A CIO who wants something stopped says so, and an ABeam reviewer does it with a reason on the record — governance, not a kill switch.
This is the whole answer to "what are you reading from our system?" — four feeds, 35 fields, no writes, every line with an owner and an end date. It is also the view that makes the next access request easy to say yes to.
